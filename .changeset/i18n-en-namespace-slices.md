[breaking] An app that sets `InternationalizationContext` directly — rendering `<InternationalizationContext value={…}>` (or its `.Provider`) instead of `InternationalizationProvider` — no longer drives `useLocale`, `useDirection` and `useCollator`, nor the components that read the locale or direction through them (the date, time and number inputs, Calendar, Chat, PowerSearch, Table sorting, Timestamp). Its strings still follow such a value through `useTranslator()` until the components move to their own slices, which stops that too. Migration: mount `InternationalizationProvider`; its `locale`, `messages` and `overrides` take the value's fields of the same name, and its `dir` takes the value's `direction`. The provider has no `translate` input: an app that supplied its own `translate` expresses those strings as `messages` (a catalog per locale) or `overrides` (sparse, per locale) and the provider resolves them in the documented order. `astryx upgrade` runs `migrate-internationalization-context`, which performs the rewrite and leaves a `translate` key behind a TODO. The provider now writes an internal runtime context that those hooks read. Reading the public context is unchanged, so a component that reads it with `useContext` and re-mounts `InternationalizationProvider` with a derived value keeps working: `locale`, `direction`, `messages`, `overrides` and `translate`, which still resolves every `@astryx.*` key against the whole shipped English catalog, with or without a provider; `useTranslator()` is unchanged. Groundwork for components carrying only their own English strings: the English catalog is also emitted one namespace at a time, the resolver takes its English fallback per call, and an internal `useComponentTranslator(slice)` lets a core component render with its own slice. The slices are not importable.
